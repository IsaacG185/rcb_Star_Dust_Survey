[Progress Report]

1. COLOUR TERM FIX
------------------
What: Plates where the colour term switched on were 0.5 to 1.3 mag too
      bright. The slope was 2 to 3 times too big, and the target never
      got its colour added back.
Fix:  Least-squares slope, kept only if clearly significant, and a fixed
      colour of 0.3 for the target.
Result: Agreement with DASCH (within 0.5 mag) 74% -> 84%.
Where: Cell 1, functions global_calibration / calibrate_target
       (COLOR_FIT = 'ols', COLOR_C = 0.3).


2. GUARDS (STOP BAD POINTS BEING PUBLISHED)
-------------------------------------------
What: Three rules turn bad measurements into limits or low confidence:
  - Plate limit: fainter than DASCH's limit - 0.5 becomes a limit.
  - Pick distance: object more than 10 px from the target is dropped
    (it was usually a neighbour).
  - Reach: more than 2 mag brighter than the brightest calibrator is
    dropped.
Result: Agreement 88% -> 91% on 10,463 plates, with 19% fewer points
        (17,694 -> 14,381), mostly wrong ones.
Where: Cell 1 settings PLATE_LIMIT_GUARD, PICK_REJECT_PX = 10,
       REACH_REJECT_GAP = 2.0.


3. HONEST ERROR BARS
--------------------
What: Quoted errors were 2 to 5 times too big. Example: quoted 0.38 mag,
      real scatter 0.15.
Fix:  Errors recalibrated against same-night plate pairs (5,365 pairs,
      13 stars).
Result: Pull sd went from 0.2-0.5 to 0.6-0.9 (honest = 1). Still a bit
        conservative on purpose.
Where: Cell 1, ERROR_CALIBRATION = True. Columns in lightcurve.csv:
       magnitude_error (calibrated), magnitude_error_quoted (old).


4. EDGE SHIFT
-------------
What: Targets just brighter than the brightest calibrator (flag 'edge')
      came out about 0.30 mag too faint vs DASCH.
Fix:  Published edge points made 0.25 mag brighter.
Result: S Aps 87% -> 90%; R CrB 85% -> 97%. RY Sgr got slightly worse.
Where: Cell 1, EDGE_SHIFT = 0.25 (column mag_unshifted = original).


5. DASCH BLEND ON V854 CEN
--------------------------
What: DASCH's flat band near 12 mag is not V854 Cen (B=8.3). It is a
      neighbour (B=12.0) 33" (23 px) south.
Evidence: On 514 DASCH-accepted plates the picked object was over 10 px
      away, with median magnitude about 12.1 (neighbour = 12.02). On
      plates where we picked the target, DASCH scatter was large (std
      2.1), as expected for the real star.
Result: "Accepted" does not remove blends. SISaP drops those plates.
Show:  V854_Cen/02_A/sisap_dasch_stacked.png; V854_Cen/02_A/
       apass_field.csv (the two stars); any image in V854_Cen/DASCH/
       cutouts.

7. SAME-NIGHT PRECISION VS DASCH
--------------------------------
What: Pairs of plates less than 0.5 day apart, same plates for both
      methods. Scatter of one measurement = robust sd of differences
      / sqrt 2. Independent of DASCH being "right", but blind to
      errors that repeat (blends).
Result (3,608 pairs): SISaP 0.114 mag, DASCH 0.121 (ratio 0.94).
  Better:  SU Tau 0.074 vs 0.140, V2552 Oph 0.046 vs 0.083,
           UW Cen 0.054 vs 0.068, RS Tel 0.097 vs 0.117.
  Equal:   Y Mus, V_CrA, V348 Sgr, V854 Cen (ratio 0.94 to 0.96).
  Worse:   RY Sgr 0.411 vs 0.175 (crowded), S Aps 0.177 vs 0.117,
           U Aqr 0.155 vs 0.119, V581 CrA (22 pairs only).
  High-confidence points beat DASCH on 8 of 9 stars.
Where: repeatability_cell.py (read-only; reads each star's
       02_A/lightcurve.csv).

9. THE BRIGHT END (R CRB, XX CAM)
---------------------------------
What: Their maxima (7 to 9 mag) are 3 to 4 mag brighter than any
      usable calibrator. APASS has nothing brighter than V=10, and our
      brightest calibrator in the R CrB cutout is about B=12.8.
Result: R CrB: 37 points, all fainter than 12.7 mag, 97% agreement on
        34 shared plates, tracking the dust declines; 90% of DASCH's
        414 points are bright and come out as limits (correctly). XX
        Cam: 1 point.
Tried and failed: Gaia as reference (R CrB 74% -> 41%), extrapolating
        past the calibrators (off by 1.5 to 3 mag), size vs magnitude,
        forced photometry (about 1.2 mag too bright on V854 Cen),
        binned full plates (0.4 to 0.55 mag scatter).
Show:  R_CrB/02_A/sisap_dasch_stacked.png; limits in R_CrB/02_A/
       limits.csv.


10. WHY DASCH DOES BETTER AT THE BRIGHT END
-------------------------------------------
  - It calibrates on the whole plate, split into 9 rings with 100+
    calibrators each. Our cutout has a handful.
  - It extends the calibration curve in a straight line past the
    saturation point, down to mag 6, and flags those points as
    extrapolated.
  - It fits a plate-wide saturation model on thousands of stars.
  - It uses ATLAS where APASS runs out.
Caveat: those bright values are extrapolated, and DASCH saturates near
        8th mag, so maxima are underestimated.


11. Dr. Clayton FWHM Method
-------------------
Why it doesn't work for us:
  - His three calibrators are 11 to 12.4 mag, but the star is much
    brighter, so it extrapolates.
  - Our own size-vs-magnitude test failed (Spearman -0.12 XX Cam, -0.68
    R CrB, -0.62 RY Sgr).
  - It is manual (positions read off in ds9), so it can't run on
    thousands of plates.
How we could use it:
  - As a spot check on a few bright plates against DASCH and our limits.
  - If he can show it is accurate at the bright end, we could automate 
  it. Dr. Clayton notebook doesn’t tell us how he checked, and I 
  haven’t seen a comparison against anything at the bright end.
   Need: 
   - Pick stars in the same cutouts that are as bright as R CrB 
   at maximum (7 to 9 mag) and have a trusted catalogue magnitude. 
   Candidates are Tycho-2 or Hipparcos B, or Gaia-derived values. 
   APASS has nothing there, so these would need a conversion to B, 
   adding roughly 0.1 to 0.2 mag of uncertainty.
   - Treat each as the unknown “target”. Fit his three faint 
   calibrators, read off the star’s magnitude, and 
   compare with the catalogue.
   - The scatter and any bias tell us directly whether the 
   extrapolation works. This is the test that matches how he uses it.


12. STARNET (MACHINE LEARNING)
------------------------------
What: A small CNN trained on APASS stars cut from the real plates
      (never on DASCH). It outputs a magnitude, its own error, the star
      position and a star / not-star probability. Merged with SISaP into
      an "improved" light curve.
Status: Tested on synthetic plates only (validation scatter 0.05 to
        0.09 mag). Real run waits for the other computer.
Where: 03_A_PyTorch.ipynb + rcb_starnet.py. Nothing to show yet.
